> and so that the project is not improvising an agreement in a pull-request comment. Do not rely on
> it as legal advice, and expect the wording to change before it is first countersigned. If you are
> contributing on an employer's time, have your employer read it too.

This agreement is between you ("You") and Platten Industries, Sean Patrick Platten, LaFayette,
Georgia, United States ("the Project"), and it covers any contribution you submit to SPLAT.

## Why this exists

SPLAT is published under the [AGPL-3.0](LICENSE) and is also offered to customers under a separate
commercial licence ([COMMERCIAL.md](COMMERCIAL.md)). Both offers must cover the whole work. If a
contribution were licensed to the Project only under the AGPL, the Project could not include it in a
commercial licence, and the contribution could not be merged. This agreement fixes that, and it is
the only reason it exists.

## 1. You keep your copyright

You retain full ownership of your contribution. You may use it elsewhere, relicense it to anyone
else, and publish it however you like. Nothing here is an assignment or a transfer.

## 2. Copyright licence you grant

You grant the Project a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free, irrevocable licence to
reproduce, prepare derivative works of, publicly display, publicly perform, sublicense and
distribute your contribution and such derivative works, **under the AGPL-3.0 and under other licence
terms of the Project's choosing, including proprietary and commercial terms.**

That last clause is the operative one. It is what lets the Project sell a commercial licence to a
work that includes your code.

## 3. Patent licence you grant

You grant the Project and recipients of software distributed by the Project a perpetual, worldwide,
non-exclusive, royalty-free, irrevocable patent licence to make, have made, use, offer to sell,
sell, import and otherwise transfer your contribution, limited to those patent claims you own or
control that are necessarily infringed by your contribution alone or by its combination with the
project it was submitted to.

If you institute patent litigation against anyone alleging that the project, or a contribution
incorporated in it, infringes a patent, the patent licences granted to you under this agreement
terminate as of the date the litigation is filed.

## 4. What you are representing

- The contribution is your original work, or you have the right to submit it under this agreement.
- If any part is not your original work, you have identified it in your submission along with its
  licence and any restriction on it, and you have the right to submit it.
- Your contribution does not knowingly include code you are aware infringes someone else's rights.
- If your employer has rights in work you create, you have either obtained their permission, or your
  employer has signed this agreement, or the contribution was made outside the scope of that
  employment.
- You are legally entitled to grant the licences above. If you are under 18, a parent or guardian
  must sign.

## 5. No obligation, and no warranty

The Project is not required to use your contribution. You provide it **as is**, without warranty of
any kind, express or implied, including fitness for a particular purpose and non-infringement.

You will not be paid for your contribution unless a separate written agreement says otherwise.

## 6. Keeping it accurate

If anything you represented above stops being true, tell the Project at
**info@plattenindustries.com**.

## 7. Governing law

to change on legal review.

## Signing it

Email **info@plattenindustries.com** with the subject `CLA`, containing:

```
I have read CLA.md at commit <the commit you read> and I agree to it.

Full name:
GitHub username:
Email:
Employer (or "none"):
Date:
```

You will get a countersigned copy back. One signature covers all your future contributions to this
project; you do not sign again per patch.

**Every commit also needs a `Signed-off-by` line** certifying the Developer Certificate of Origin.
`git commit -s` adds it. The DCO and this agreement do different jobs: the DCO says you had the
right to send the code, and this says the Project may license it commercially. Both are required for
code. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## If you would rather not sign

That is a reasonable position, and it costs you nothing here. Bug reports, reproductions,
measurements, security findings and contradicting evidence are all welcome with no agreement of any
kind, and this project values a measurement that falsifies one of its claims above a patch.
