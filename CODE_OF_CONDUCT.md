# Code of conduct

This project is small and technical. The standard is short on purpose.

## Expected

- **Argue about the work, not the person.** "This measurement does not support that claim" is
  always welcome, however bluntly it is put. "You are an idiot" is not the same sentence.
- **Say what you measured.** Disagreement backed by a command and its output is the most valuable
  thing anyone can bring here, and it wins over a document every time.
- **Be willing to be wrong in public.** Claims in this project have been withdrawn after passing,
  more than once, because the thing they proved changed underneath them. That is the normal cost of
  working this way, not an embarrassment.
- **Assume the other person is short of time**, not acting in bad faith.

## Not accepted

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained hostility toward anyone, in issues, pull requests, commit
  messages, or any channel where this project is discussed.
- Personal attacks, demeaning remarks, or abuse directed at someone's identity — including race,
  sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, age, religion, nationality or
  appearance.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their consent.
- Using this project's channels to coordinate attacks on systems, people or organisations. SPLAT is
  a tool for running agents against live platforms; help with doing that to systems you are not
  authorised to touch is not in scope here and will not be given.

## Scope

This applies in the repository, in anything linked from it, and in other venues when someone is
representing the project.

## Reporting

**info@plattenindustries.com.** Reports go to one person, Sean Patrick Platten, who maintains this
project. You will get an acknowledgement within 7 days.

If your report concerns the maintainer, say so plainly; there is no committee to escalate to, and it
is better to have it on the record than unsaid. Reports stay confidential to the extent that acting
on them allows, and nobody gets retaliated against for making one in good faith.

## Consequences

In order, depending on what happened: a private word, a public correction, removal of a comment or
commit, a temporary block, a permanent ban. Severity and repetition decide; a first-time
misjudgement handled gracefully is not a ban.

Nothing here is a reason to reject a technical contribution that is correct. A patch is judged on
whether it holds up. Conduct is handled as conduct.
